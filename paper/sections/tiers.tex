\section{Tiers and Distribution}\label{sec:tiers}

\subsection{One Library}\label{sec:library}
Applications use RandAO through one Lua library. Code that already calls \texttt{math.random} needs one line:
\begin{verbatim}
rng.install(msg)    -- math.random now draws a deterministic stream
\end{verbatim}
When a draw carries value, the same logic moves to a network tier. Every request names a continuation that receives a deterministic random stream, so moving a call site between tiers changes one option and nothing else:
\begin{verbatim}
rng.request(msg, { tier = "timelock", on = "Loot-Roll" })
\end{verbatim}
A stream offers integers without modulo bias, floats, picks and shuffles, and draws the same numbers on every node and every replay.

\subsection{The Tiers}\label{sec:tier_table}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{2.2cm} p{4.2cm} p{3.6cm} p{1.8cm} p{1.4cm}}
\textbf{Tier} & \textbf{Source} & \textbf{Who can cheat} & \textbf{Latency} & \textbf{Price} \\
\hline
\texttt{local} & SHA-256 stream over the message & the sender, by re-signing & none & free \\
\texttt{quick} & threshold-BLS round, $t$ of $n$ validators & $t$ colluding validators & $\sim$1~s & $t+1$ RNG \\
\texttt{timelock} & time-lock commit-reveal of every validator & all validators together & a few seconds & $n+1$ RNG \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The \texttt{local} tier needs no network and suits cosmetic effects and low-value draws. The \texttt{quick} tier answers in about a second and suits frequent game events. The \texttt{timelock} tier carries RandAO's any-honest guarantee and suits anything worth attacking: lotteries, rare drops, tournament brackets, validator selection.

\subsection{Distribution}\label{sec:distribution}
The aim is for safe randomness to be what developers get without thinking about it. The plan:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Arweave module}: the library is published on Arweave as a HyperBEAM Lua module. A process lists it in its \texttt{module} map and calls \texttt{require("rng")}.
    \item \textbf{APM}: the library is published on APM, AO's package manager.
    \item \textbf{aos and hyper-aos}: we will propose the library to the aos and hyper-aos maintainers as the safe default behind \texttt{math.random}.
    \item \textbf{HyperBEAM}: the \texttt{timelock@1.0} device will be proposed upstream to HyperBEAM once it has passed its soak test on our nodes.
\end{itemize}
